# Task 0 — Referral Feedback One-Pager

Referred Simplify Money to 3 friends. Asked each to install the app, complete the profile, use it for a few days, and share their honest feedback.

---

## Riya (Working professional, Android user)

**Setup:** Smooth until the Gmail connection step. Took around 3 minutes overall. She liked that the app clearly showed what data it had not accessed after connecting Gmail.

**What she liked:**

- The month-over-month spending comparison, especially insights such as "₹957 under your usual." She said this was one of the most useful parts of the app.
- Kuber.AI felt natural when used in Hindi. She asked where her money was going and received a useful response.
- The dark theme felt polished and gave the app a premium appearance.

**What she didn't like:**

- "The app wants SMS, Gmail, AND my bank login. I gave SMS and Gmail but I'm not linking my bank. That's where I stopped." She did not complete the full setup.
- The Financial Health score showed 605 but did not explain the scale. "605 out of what? Is this like CIBIL? I couldn't understand it."
- After opening the app again two days later, she had not received any useful notifications or reminders. "I forgot it existed. If it's tracking my money, tell me something useful."
- The "Invest your surplus" button on Runway felt too promotional. Her expectation was primarily spending tracking rather than investment recommendations.

---

## Rohit (College student, Android user)

**Setup:** Had trouble with transaction detection because the bank SMS format was not fully recognized. Only 11 transactions were detected even though he expected more than 25.

**What he liked:**

- The gamification aspect. The 605 score made him curious about improving it even though the scoring system was not clear.
- Digital gold starting from ₹10 was an interesting feature and felt different from typical spending trackers.
- Category detection worked well for common food transactions such as Swiggy and Zomato.

**What he didn't like:**

- "More than half my transactions are missing. If I can't trust the total, the whole app is pointless." He felt the missing transactions made the overall data unreliable.
- The Learn section contained modules and quizzes, but the content felt generic. He expected advice that was more relevant to his own spending.
- He tried adding a cash expense manually. The functionality worked, but the entry point was not prominent enough. Since he uses cash frequently, he wanted manual entry to be much faster.
- The insurance and digital-gold sections felt unrelated to his original reason for installing the app. He expected the core spending-tracking experience to remain the main focus.

---

## Karan (Freelancer, multiple financial accounts)

**Setup:** One bank account connected successfully. Another payment account was not available in the supported institution list.

**What he liked:**

- The "unlink and delete everything" option in Settings increased his confidence because he knew he could remove his connected data.
- The month-over-month spending chart was useful without requiring multiple navigation steps.
- Kuber.AI was able to answer questions such as how much he had spent on food and provide a meaningful number.

**What he didn't like:**

- One of his frequently used payment accounts was not supported. Because part of his freelance income went through that account, he felt the app only represented part of his financial activity.
- The Runway page displayed "NEEDS ATTENTION" but did not clearly explain what action was required. He wanted a specific recommendation rather than a generic warning.
- Freelance income was grouped together instead of allowing income to be associated with individual clients or projects. For a freelancer, this makes it harder to understand where payments came from.
- His connected credit card showed no statement information after several days. The lack of an error message made it difficult to understand whether syncing was still in progress or had failed.

---

## Summary — What They Didn't Like (Common Across All 3)

### 1. Too many permissions before showing value

All three users were hesitant about the combination of SMS, Gmail, and bank access.

A better approach could be to show some useful functionality after the first permission instead of requiring users to complete several connections before experiencing the core value.

### 2. Incomplete bank support breaks trust

Missing transactions directly affect users' confidence in the numbers shown by the app.

If a bank's SMS format is not fully supported, users may see incomplete totals and question the reliability of the entire product.

### 3. Feature sprawl dilutes the core experience

The app combines spending tracking with insurance, digital gold, investments, financial education, and other features.

The feedback suggested that users primarily wanted the spending-tracking experience to be accurate and useful before being introduced to additional financial products.

### 4. Financial scores need clearer context

The Financial Health score attracted attention, but users did not immediately understand what the number represented or how it was calculated.

Showing the score's scale, meaning, and the actions that can improve it could make the feature more useful.

### 5. Users want actionable notifications

After connecting their financial data, users expected the app to proactively surface useful information.

Instead of only displaying dashboards, notifications could highlight meaningful changes, unusual spending, upcoming payments, or other relevant financial events.
